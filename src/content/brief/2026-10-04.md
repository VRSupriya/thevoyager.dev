Sunday's thread is boring in the best way: nobody shipped a miracle, but several people quietly decided what an agent is *not* allowed to do.

## 🔥 Apple wants a harder "yes" before an AI agent can read your whole Mac
Apple says it will add protections around Full Disk Access, the macOS permission that exposes files, mail, messages and browsing history. Its reasoning is that agents make an already extraordinary level of access riskier. No dates or details yet, only the direction.

If you ship a desktop agent, assume the one-click grant is on its way out. Design for the narrower path now: ask for specific folders, not the whole disk.
[Source](https://www.theapplepost.com/2026/10/03/72916/apple-plans-tougher-mac-full-disk-access-controls-as-ai-agents-expand/)

## 🚀 A German-English open model that's cheap to serve
Aleph Alpha released Kolibri-1 under Apache 2.0. It's a mixture-of-experts model (only some "experts" fire per token) with 78.1B parameters in total but 3.46B active, and a 1M-token context window. They report 96.9% on AIME 2025 and say it handles 18 concurrent 256k-token queries on two H100s.

Those are the lab's own numbers, so treat them as a claim to test. For regulated or on-prem work where data can't leave the building, it's worth a look.
[Source](https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/)

## 🤖 Uber's answer to "what do we do with 800 MCP servers?"
Uber runs more than 800 MCP servers exposing about 5,000 tools. A registry holds the catalogue, and a proxy translates agent calls into Uber's internal protocols. A crawler drafts tool descriptions from existing service definitions, but every tool starts **disabled** until its owning team switches it on.

The neat trick is a single proxy exposing four discovery tools, so an agent doesn't load thousands of schemas into its context. Off-by-default plus search-on-demand is a pattern you can borrow at any size.
[Source](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/designing-mcp-gateway/)

## 🔬 "Done" isn't the same as "correct"
ThinkingBox, from Microsoft and Hugging Face, grades agents by what they leave in the database rather than what they say. Across 507 business-workflow tasks and 18 models, about 67% of failed runs ended cleanly and called state-changing tools, yet still left wrong data. Kimi-K3 solved 93.89% of tasks at least once but only 13.41% every time out of 20.

Run your own evals more than once. A single pass rate hides the thing you actually care about, which is whether it works on Tuesday too.
[Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/microsoft/thinkingbox)

## ✍️ Simon Willison on the bill you didn't mean to run up
Simon argues cloud services and pay-per-use APIs should stop at a hard budget cap by default, with an opt-out checkbox for people who want the risk. Warning emails aren't enough when an agent can spend overnight. He points to AWS spending limits (September 2026) and Google Cloud's Spend Caps (July) as steps in that direction.

It's a small design rule with a big payoff: give every agent its own capped key.
[Source](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/)

## 🏗️ Istio 1.31 puts an agent-aware proxy in the mesh, and moves house
Istio 1.31 lets you run agentgateway, a Rust data plane that understands MCP as well as HTTP, as a waypoint in ambient mode. It also stops publishing to Google Cloud registries as the project moves to AWS, because of a change in its funding model.

Check your image and Helm sources: a test outage is scheduled for October 13 (15:00–18:00 UTC), and the old endpoints retire in December.
[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/istio-1-31-agentgateway/)
