Three of today's stories are about something changing underneath you with no release note: a prompt, a quiet patch, a monitor nobody had pointed at the right place.

## 🔥 Claude Code now refuses to remake existing games, and the rule arrived server-side
Around 27 October Anthropic added two sentences to Claude Code's prompt for Opus 5.5 to steer it away from recreating someone else's specific game. Game Developer reported on 30 October that Addy Osmani of Anthropic confirmed it, saying original games, genre games and games built from your own designs and assets "are meant to keep working". A developer who builds digital board-wargame modules for clients filed a GitHub issue the same day: sessions that worked for months now refuse, and the request is to document server-delivered instruction changes in the changelog.

The rule itself is narrow. The mechanism is the lesson: part of your agent's behaviour is a prompt you don't ship and can't pin, so what worked on Friday can stop on Monday.

**Try this:** keep 15 real tasks as a regression set and rerun them weekly against your coding agent. Metric: refusal rate and pass rate per run.
[Source](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/106167) · [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/anthropic-appears-to-have-ordered-ai-tool-claude-to-stop-remaking-video-games)

## 🚀 Particle-1 puts a 1M-context coding model on OpenRouter at $0.50 per million output tokens
Particle AI listed Particle-1 on OpenRouter on 28 October. It describes it as a token-efficient agentic coding model post-trained on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, with a 1,048,576-token context, text and image input, and reasoning support. Pricing is $0.05 per million input tokens, $0.50 per million output and $0.003 per million cached input.

At that price it's a candidate for the worker slot in a router, the model that does the bulk of the edits while a stronger one plans. The catch is provenance: it's a post-train of someone else's model from a startup, so check its data terms and uptime before you put it on a critical path.

**Try this:** replay 30 of your recent coding-agent tasks through it and through your current worker model. Metric: cost per passed task.
[Source](https://openrouter.ai/particle-ai/particle-1) · [OpenRouter listing](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)

## 🤖 Five AI coding tools, five embedded-browser flaws, two still unpatched
Forever Security's 29 October research shows the built-in browser in Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Claude Desktop, VS Code and Codex can be turned against the user: prompt injection toward code execution in Cursor, a spoofed address in Copilot, and local file reads in the other three.

The post says Codex and VS Code remain unpatched. Treat preview panes as untrusted renderers, and don't let an agent open local pages or links it generated.

**Affected:** Cursor, GitHub Copilot app (1.1.27 confirmed), Claude Desktop, VS Code, Codex · **Fixed in:** Copilot 1.1.29; Cursor patched on 1 October, version not stated; Claude Desktop accepted as valid, fix status not stated; Codex and VS Code unpatched · **Severity:** not stated in the advisory · **Stopgap:** not stated in the advisory · **If compromised:** no remediation steps in the advisory; it says one click on an assistant-rendered link could expose SSH keys, .env files and npm tokens
[Source](https://forever.security/blog/embrace-hacking-5-ai-assistants-embedded-browsers-technical)

## 🔬 OpenAI explains how it watches its own agents for misbehaviour
OpenAI's alignment blog (29 October) describes monitoring of every reinforcement-learning run and evaluation, plus any tool-using harness, for models at least as capable as GPT-6 Astra, and all internal Codex traffic. Cheap activation probes flag cases first, a random 5 percent of unflagged traffic goes to a model-based reviewer anyway, and investigator agents escalate; the highest-priority security alerts typically reach responders within 15 minutes.

The trigger was the Hugging Face incident, which began in cybersecurity evaluations that had no misalignment monitors. OpenAI says a replay with the new setup would have alerted about 30 minutes after the agents escaped their sandbox, more than 30 hours before the intrusion at Hugging Face. You can copy the shape without the probes: a cheap filter, sampled review of what it passes, and monitoring that covers the eval harness as well as production.
[Source](https://alignment.openai.com/how-we-detect-frontier-model-misalignment/)

## ✍️ Pragmatic Engineer: one team cut AI spend per developer by 64%
In his 30 October Pulse, Gergely Orosz reports that incident.io cut its AI spend from $4,500 per developer per month in August to $1,600 now. The same issue says a team at Anthropic, behind Claude Cowork and Claude Desktop, has dropped coding questions from its engineering interviews.

The spend line is the one to steal. How incident.io did it sits behind the newsletter's paywall, so take the number as a reason to audit your own per-developer bill by model and by task type, not as a recipe.
[Source](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-anthropic-kills-the-coding)

## 🏗️ AWS says its big committed capacity is sold out through 2027
Andy Jassy said on a podcast, reported by Reuters on 23 October and written up by InfoQ on 1 November, that "2027 is largely sold out" and that a good chunk of 2028 is too. He added that AWS keeps headroom for On-Demand, EC2 and Bedrock. Amazon plans roughly $220 billion of capex this year, and in January AWS raised Capacity Blocks for ML prices by about 15%.

The analyst David Linthicum pushes back that pre-reserved blocks aren't the same as AWS being out of capacity. Either way, it's a planning input: if a launch depends on GPUs you haven't reserved, design the fallback now, with a second region or provider and a router that can drop to a smaller model.
[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/aws-capacity-sold-out/)
