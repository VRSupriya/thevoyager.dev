Today's thread is access. Stolen keys turned into a bill, and the more useful agent designs this week are the ones that never hold a key at all.

## 🔥 Railway's breach went after AI keys, with about $740,000 in misuse
Railway's full report, published 10 November, says an attacker used an unknown build-infrastructure flaw to partially escape filesystem isolation on 20 October, then decrypted variables, "prioritizing AI provider keys". Estimated unauthorized spend: about $740,000.

At most 1.85% (about 100,000) of workspaces could have had variables decrypted. A key in a deploy platform is an unmetered credit card, so spend caps and per-service keys matter.

**Try this:** pull AI-provider usage since 20 October and look for traffic you can't attribute. Baseline: normal daily spend; metric: spend from unknown keys.

**Affected:** Railway customers with variables or secrets stored on Railway (at most 1.85% of workspaces could have had variables decrypted) · **Fixed in:** no versions stated; Railway says each of the attack vectors used has been remediated and builds now run on ephemeral VMs · **Severity:** not stated in the report · **Stopgap:** Railway suggested customers rotate credentials manually and advised high-risk customers to turn off deployments · **If compromised:** report charges you didn't make via the form in the report; rotating AI provider keys is this brief's advice, not the report's

[Source](https://blog.railway.com/p/incident-report-october-security-incident) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/apple/railway-breach-leads-to-740-000-in-fraudulent-ai-charges/)

## 🚀 Gemini 4 Carbon is now available to developers and enterprises
Google's 10 November post makes Carbon available to paying developers and enterprises through Google AI Studio and Gemini for business, after a first release to cyber defenders about two weeks earlier. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20 after 30 November.

Google's own numbers say it ranks first on CursorBench 4.0 at more than 2X lower cost per task than the next best external model, and that its rate of successful attacks in Google's test fell from 3.1% on Gemini 4 Argon to 0.8%. Treat both as vendor claims. The price step on 30 November means any cost comparison you run this month needs the $4/$20 line next to it.

[Source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/)

## 🤖 Teleport Beams reaches GA: agents that start with no standing privileges
Teleport made Beams generally available on 9 November. Each agent runs in an ephemeral micro-VM with its traffic captured, gets secretless access to cloud accounts, Kubernetes, PostgreSQL and MCP servers, and has no standing privileges, and a human or an LLM judge reviews and revises each execution plan. A global kill switch comes with it.

That moves agent security from "which key do we hand it" to "which grant does this run need". It's the inverse of the Railway problem above. The trade-off is a new control plane in the path of every agent action, so test its latency on your slowest workflow first.

[Source](https://goteleport.com/about/newsroom/press-releases/teleport-beams-trusted-runtime-ai-agents-general-availability/)

## 🔬 Under ten reprogrammed neurons can backdoor an agent, Microsoft Research shows
Microsoft's 10 November research post describes "Lazarus": rewriting fewer than 10 dormant neurons in an LLM, with no retraining, is enough to backdoor an agent built on it. It is a research claim. The model passes standard evaluations until a trigger such as a date change appears, then runs an attacker's command with the agent's own access.

Behaviour tests can't clear a model like that. Reliable detection needs the original, unmodified weights to compare against, so provenance becomes part of the design: pin open-weight models to their publisher, keep agent permissions narrow, and review consequential actions before they run. Microsoft says signed models and weight scanning are planned, not shipped.

[Source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/)

## 🏗️ NVIDIA Snapshot restores GPU workloads in seconds, not minutes
NVIDIA's 10 November post introduces Snapshot, an open-source checkpoint and restore tool for GPU workloads on Kubernetes. On B200 GPUs it restored models in 3.6 to 42.7 seconds, 8 to 36 times faster than a cold start; the largest MoE models take 6 to 19 minutes cold. Those are NVIDIA's numbers, on vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM.

For autoscaling, cold start is the hidden floor under your latency promise. Restores need the same GPU model and driver as the checkpoint, so heterogeneous fleets get less out of it.

**Try this:** checkpoint your biggest model once and compare restore time against a cold start on the same node. Metric: seconds to first served token.

[Source](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/start-and-restore-gpu-workloads-in-seconds-with-nvidia-snapshot/)
