Today's thread is boundaries: between tenants on a disk, between a country and its data, between an agent and the tools it can call. Two of the six are reminders that old infrastructure details still decide new AI risks.

## 🔥 Cloudflare Containers let tenants read each other's old disk blocks
A researcher found that Cloudflare Containers on shared hosts could expose up to 60 KiB of a previous customer's data. The cause was dm-thin with 64 KiB blocks and `skip_block_zeroing` on: a 4 KiB write allocated a whole block but only overwrote its own part. Cloudflare says it found residue on 18 of 24 placements and 20 of 22 nodes, recovered intact SQLite databases, and saw no evidence of exploitation.
If you run agent sandboxes or code-execution tools on shared hosts, ask your provider how disks are zeroed and reused, because this is exactly the layer a prompt-injected agent will probe. Cloudflare fixed it fleet-wide by September 7 and cleaned up by September 19, with nothing for customers to do.
[Source](https://blog.cloudflare.com/containers-cross-tenant-vulnerability/)

## 🚀 Kolibri is a sovereign MoE you can actually host
Aleph Alpha released Kolibri on October 3 as open weights under Apache 2.0. It's an English–German MoE with 78.1B total and 3.46B active parameters, 384 experts (6 active), and up to 1M tokens of context. They say it serves 18 concurrent 256k-token queries on two H100s, and they report 96.9% on AIME 2025 and 84.3% on GPQA Diamond.
Those are vendor numbers, so rerun them on your own German documents before believing them. The real draw is for regulated buyers who need a model whose training data, weights and runtime all sit under their control, and the "abstain when evidence is thin" training is worth testing against your RAG failure cases.
[Source](https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/)

## 🤖 What Uber learned running 800 MCP servers
Uber described its MCP Gateway: a registry as control plane and a proxy as data plane that turns Thrift, Protobuf and HTTP endpoints into MCP servers. A crawler generates tool definitions, but they start disabled until the owning team enables them. Authorization is per tool, and responses get PII redaction. It now covers 800+ servers and 5,000+ tools.
The part to copy is how they handled context bloat: incremental discovery, GraphQL-style field projection, and a code mode that writes results to files instead of into the prompt. At this scale the gateway matters more than any individual server, and enable-by-default is the mistake to avoid.
[Source](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/designing-mcp-gateway/)

## 🔬 Agent memory that reads 8% of the history
APDMem, accepted to EMNLP 2026's industry track, stores conversation memory in four layers: themes, key facts, turn-level evidence notes and raw messages. A controller starts at the top and only drops down when the query needs detail. The authors report strong LongMemEval results while touching about 8% of the conversation data.
The idea is sensible for cost and latency, since most queries shouldn't pay for raw transcripts. The open questions are the extra controller calls and what happens when a summary layer drifts from the raw text; I'd want that measured before trusting it.
[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02472)

## ✍️ Andrew Ng on open-weight cyber capability
In his October 2 letter, Ng reads Anthropic's analysis of GLM-5.3 as good news. The open model hit 12% on a subset of ExploitBench tasks versus 14% for Claude Mythos, and about $20 of GLM-5.3-Flash tokens found a Chrome flaw. He argues defenders get cheap, capable tooling and that this is an engineering problem to fix, not a reason to panic.
He's candid about a caveat: on the full benchmark the gap is 54.4% versus 78%. The practical read is that attack cost is falling, so patch cadence and automated scanning in your own pipelines matter more than the debate.
[Source](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-373)

## 🏗️ Claude on Bedrock now stays in India
AWS says Claude Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and Haiku 4.5 are available through an India geographic inference profile that routes only between Mumbai (ap-south-1) and Hyderabad (ap-south-2). Data stays in the source region, billing and quotas are tracked there, and logging in CloudWatch and CloudTrail happens in the source region only.
Prompts and outputs can still move between the two Indian regions, so check that against your residency wording. It's also reachable only through the bedrock-runtime endpoint, so clients on other paths won't get it.
[Source](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/amazon-bedrock-expands-claude-model-availability-to-india-cross-region-inference/)
