Most of today's news is about the layer around the model: who sells it, how it talks, and what happens to it when a machine dies. The model itself barely changed.

## 🔥 OpenAI's GPT-6 models are coming to Google Cloud
Google Cloud says three OpenAI models, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, are rolling out in private preview to select enterprise customers in Model Garden. Google calls itself the only hyperscaler offering all three leading closed-source model families. They're sold through Google Cloud Marketplace, so usage lands on one invoice and counts against existing commitments.

If you buy models through a cloud, this changes the procurement conversation more than the architecture. Zero data retention and Confidential Compute on dedicated infrastructure are both "coming soon", and pricing isn't published, so regulated workloads should wait. Abstract your model client now and you can compare it with the others on your own evals when access opens.

[Source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/google-cloud-brings-openai-models-to-customers/)

## 🚀 ElevenLabs ships a realtime voice model you can swap in
Eleven Realtime comes in two sizes, Max and Mini, both speech-to-speech models for voice agents, with 90+ languages. ElevenLabs reports time to first audio of 0.57 s for Max and 0.62 s for Mini. On Artificial Analysis evaluations cited in its post, Max scores 98.8% on Big Bench Audio and 87.1% on tool use; Mini drops to 75.9% and 60.3%.

The API is WebSocket-only and follows the OpenAI Realtime format, so migrating can be close to an endpoint change. The gaps matter: no WebRTC, no published pricing, and the benchmark gap says Mini is for simple flows, not tool-heavy ones.

**Try this:** replay 50 recorded calls through your current realtime model and Max. Compare tool-call success rate, with latency as the second metric.

[Source](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/realtime)

## 🤖 Walmart opens an MCP gateway so agents can shop for customers
Walmart announced that outside agents will be able to shop on a customer's behalf. Agent Gateway, built on MCP, starts as a beta for companies and developers with no date for general availability, and Agent Profiles are due "in the next month". Named integrations are Muse, ChatGPT and Gemini, alongside Walmart's own Sparky. Walmart's terms say agents may not act beyond what the shopper intended.

That last rule is the interesting one. A retailer is writing the permission model for third-party agents before there's a standard for it, and "shopper intent" isn't something you can enforce in code. Expect every merchant API you depend on to grow something similar.

[Source](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)

## 🔬 Train an agent across 18 harnesses, not one
A new preprint, Harness Scaling, splits a harness into five parts (system prompt, tool interface, observation, context management, workflow) with three variants each, giving 243 configurations. It trains on 18 of them, picked by an orthogonal design. On a Qwen3-30B-A3B model, the average score rises from 37.29 to 42.78 with GRPO, and a transfer test to Claude Code scores 41.20 against 38.08 for the best single-harness baseline.

The takeaway for builders: a model tuned to one harness may be quietly overfit to it. No code is linked, and the gains are modest on small models, so treat it as a hypothesis worth a cheap check on your own agent.

[Source](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29458)

## ✍️ Claude Mods: plugins that sit inside the agent loop
Dan McAteer's Latent Space piece describes mods, Claude Code plugins written as TypeScript or JavaScript functions that respond to events like a prompt, a tool call or a render, and can observe, re-render or handle the event themselves. He calls them "middleware for the agent harness". Six ship built in, including a side agent called You Should Know that flags things you may have missed.

His cost note is the useful part: the Next Steps Supervisor mod is estimated at about 5-6k input-equivalent tokens per check, and it fired on 99 of 111 of his turns. The article also says mods aren't sandboxed. Middleware is powerful, and every mod is code with the agent's reach.

[Source](https://www.latent.space/p/claude-mods)

## 🏗️ Vercel makes coding-agent sessions survive a crash
Vercel open-sourced durable-harness on 19 October. It wraps existing harnesses (Pi, OpenCode, Claude Code, Codex) so each session keeps a log of the conversation and every model and tool call, with intent written before a call and the outcome after. If a function dies, another reads the log and resumes. Finished calls are read back, not re-run or re-billed.

The design question is side effects. Tools tagged replay-safe re-run, "never" tools are reported to the model as possibly done, and a call that finished just before the crash may not be logged yet. It's Apache-2.0 but on 0.0.x and marked experimental.

**Try this:** kill a worker mid-turn in a long session, with and without it. Count duplicated side effects.

[Source](https://vercel.com/blog/making-every-harness-durable) · [Repo](https://github.com/vercel-labs/durable-harness)
