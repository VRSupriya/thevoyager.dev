Most of this week's bad news came through a door someone else left open: a vendor's key, a CI token, a dependency that quietly changed.

## 🔥 Cursor's breach began with a key its vendor exposed
Cursor's update of 13 November says the breach behind the tampered Claude Code extension of 29 October started with an access key it had issued to Fireworks. Fireworks exposed it, and the attacker copied coding-agent traces from a read-only bucket the two shared, some with unredacted credentials. The fake Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 were up about 11 hours and could have reached at most 5,000 users.

A read-only key to a shared bucket was enough to get from a vendor's mistake to your marketplace. Treat agent traces as secrets, and audit what you've shared with vendors.

**Affected:** Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 from Cursor's marketplace, 29 October · **Fixed in:** not stated in the advisory (marketplace rebuilt, inference moved off Fireworks) · **Severity:** not stated in the advisory · **Stopgap:** uninstalling the extension alone may not be enough · **If compromised:** per Cursor's notice (29 October, updated 30 October), remove the unauthorized SSH key and persistence, then rotate credentials and sessions reachable from affected hosts

[Source](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update) · [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cursor-confirms-security-breach-led-to-malicious-claude-code-extension/) · [Cursor's October notice](https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice)

## 🚀 IBM trains small Granite models to ignore orders hidden in documents
IBM published two Granite 4.1 preview models, 3B and 8B, trained to treat instructions found inside user data as data rather than commands. IBM's own tests say that on the OPI benchmark they avoided 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tried and 95% more than the best open model. The 8B card lists Apache 2.0, and IBM's blog calls it "an experimental preview, ready for exploration and evaluation but not for production".

It's a learned behaviour, not an enforced guarantee, and IBM says adaptive multi-turn attacks are still being worked on. Think of it as one cheap layer in front of your tool-call boundary, never a replacement for it.

**Try this:** run your own set of documents with planted instructions through granite-4.1-8b-llmon-preview and your current small model. Count attack success rate, and watch task accuracy so you don't buy safety with quality.

[Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite) · [8B model card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-8b-llmon-preview)

## 🤖 ChatGPT Wallet lets agents pay, with a person approving each purchase
OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November. Its announcement calls it "a secure way for agents in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and dots to complete purchases with user approval", working with existing card protections. CNBC reports it is initially available to Pro subscribers in the US over 18, with the card networks providing a payment token for the purchase.

The design point is the control: an approval step and a payment token instead of a stored card. If you run a checkout, expect agent traffic, and expect your fraud tooling to have opinions about it.

[Source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/openai-chatgpt-wallet-dot-purchases.html)

## 🔬 CoReMem: coding-agent memory that counts what it costs
A preprint submitted on 12 November, CoReMem, builds a memory for successive coding tasks that values what it stores by what it will save later. On the ChainSWE benchmark, the authors report a 27B actor's resolve rate rising from 21.0% to 25.7%, with 17% fewer steps and 11% fewer total cache-aware tokens, memory-module calls included, against the same actor without memory.

Counting the memory's own calls is the honest part: plenty of memory add-ons win on accuracy and lose on the bill. One benchmark and one 27B actor, so treat it as a design to copy, not a result to bank.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17316)

## ✍️ Harrison Chase: design the goal, not just the prompt
In a LangChain post of 14 November, Chase and two colleagues from Actively AI argue that as agents take open-ended goals, the real bottleneck is learning from feedback: "The bottleneck for agents performing is often them learning from feedback." Their case is Actively AI's sales agents. Version one ran an agent per account, so nothing learned carried across; version two runs goal-oriented "motions" on Deep Agents, one per job type across many accounts, so one reviewer's feedback transfers.

The architectural point is to split agents by job, not by customer, so review effort compounds. It's one case study, not a benchmark.

[Source](https://www.langchain.com/blog/goal-engineering)

## 🏗️ Block runs people and agents through one open log, Buzz
Block's Buzz is an Apache 2.0 workspace where employees and agents share channels. Databricks' write-up of 12 November says Unity Gateway is the model-access layer behind it, so people pick frontier or open-weight models through one integration. The repo describes a Rust workspace where one relay is the single source of truth: messages, reviews, workflow steps and git events are signed events in one log, with Postgres, Redis and S3/MinIO storage, and each agent has its own key and an audit trail.

That gives two choke points to govern: identity and audit at the log, model access at the gateway. Early days: the repo says approval gates are still being wired up.

[Source](https://www.databricks.com/blog/how-block-powers-collaborative-ai-agents-buzz-and-unity-gateway) · [Buzz repo](https://github.com/block/buzz)
