The most useful number today is a date range: if prompts went through GMI Cloud on OpenRouter between 18 October and 18 November, treat what was in them as potentially exposed.

## 🔥 GMI Cloud confirms an intruder in the logs behind its OpenRouter endpoints
GMI Cloud's notice of 24 November says an unauthorized party got into a logging and monitoring system behind its inference platform, on 21–22 October and again from 27 October. About three days of prompts and responses were retained, so requests from 18 October to 18 November are affected. Test keys a researcher put in prompts were later used without authorization.

This follows OpenRouter's 22 November off-boarding of three unnamed providers. A gateway's "zero retention" label is only as good as the provider behind it, so the cheap fix is upstream: keep secrets out of prompts.

**Affected:** requests to GMI Cloud endpoints on OpenRouter between 18 October and 18 November 2026 · **Fixed in:** not stated as a version; the system was taken offline on 22 October and rebuilt, compromised credentials were revoked on 18 November, and prompt/response logging has stopped · **Severity:** not stated · **Stopgap:** the notice gives none beyond the step below · **If compromised:** rotate any credentials, API keys or tokens that were in prompts, then review recent usage

[Source](https://www.gmicloud.ai/en/blog/security-incident-affecting-gmi-cloud-endpoints-on-openrouter)

## 🚀 Nemotron 3.5 Parse learns to draft pages faster
NVIDIA's Hugging Face post (26 November) walks through its 0.6B-parameter document parser, which turns page images into structured data. Moving from supervised fine-tuning to GRPO cut pages flagged as looping from 126 to 29, and a speculative decoder called DFlash2 runs 7.62x faster at concurrency 1 on one H200. At concurrency 512 the gain shrinks to 1.52x (20.44 pages/s against 13.48).

All numbers are NVIDIA's own. The useful lesson is that the speed-up fades under load, so benchmark at the concurrency you run, not the one in the headline.

**Try this:** run 50 of your own scanned pages through Nemotron 3.5 Parse, with and without DFlash2, at your real concurrency. Baseline: your current parser. Metric: pages per second, plus pages that loop or hit the token limit.

[Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/building-on-nemotron-35-parse-open-data-fine-tunin) · [Model](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3.5-Parse)

## 🤖 Pax, Pydantic's terminal coding agent, is built from plugins
Pydantic announced Pax on 25 November, a terminal coding agent on its Pydantic AI Harness SDK. Even the coding behaviour is a plugin: `/plugins disable coder` turns it into a plain chat client, and `pax --agent my_agents:reviewer` runs any Pydantic AI agent you already wrote. Sessions can run locally or in E2B, Daytona or Modal sandboxes.

Pydantic's SWE81 runs put Pax at 58.5 on average, narrowly ahead of the other three harnesses that ran every model; they are single self-reported runs, and the post promises full methodology and reruns with 1.0. The design question for you: do you want your agent logic and your coding harness in one framework, or separate?

**Try this:** `uvx pydantic-ai-pax`, then point it at one agent you already run. Baseline: your current harness on three real tickets. Metric: tickets resolved and cost per ticket.

[Source](https://pydantic.dev/articles/pax-harness-beta) · [Docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/pax/)

## 🔬 Let the model decide when to compact its own context
A preprint from NVIDIA researchers (25 November) tests a context API the model calls itself, instead of the harness compacting at a fixed threshold. On OfficeQA Pro v2 with a 32K budget, GPT 6.1 goes from 21% to 56%, and Kimi gains accuracy with up to 41% fewer input tokens. Reusing the KV cache of the unchanged suffix after an edit cuts prefill compute by up to 15% per request.

Models differ a lot in how often they use the API, and prompt changes shift that timing, so this is a behaviour you would have to measure per model. The Hugging Face page links an NVIDIA GitHub repo.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34147) · [Hugging Face page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34147)

## 🏗️ How Dell tuned embeddings for agentic search
At QCon AI, Dell's Rachel Shalom described fixing agentic search that missed internal terms such as PowerScale and CloudIQ. The recipe: an LLM writes three questions per chunk, hard negatives are mined from the top 20 hits, and an open embedding model (Nomic Embed for Confluence, EmbeddingGemma for code) is trained with InfoNCE on 4 H100s. A golden set of 300 expert-verified questions, answers and sources scores it, and Dell re-embeds every two days.

Dell reports relative gains only: the right source found in at least 12% more cases and LLM-judged answer quality up a little over 4%. OpenAI embeddings weren't benchmarked because Dell doesn't allow sending that data to OpenAI. The modest gain is the honest part: a data-governance constraint shaped the model choice.

[Source](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/fine-tune-enterprise-rag-embeddings/)

**🛡️ Security & governance**
- **LiteLLM MCP stdio command injection** — ZDI's advisory (public 18 November, updated 24 November) rates CVE-2026-136535 at CVSS 7.2 with authentication required; the vendor disputes it, and from v1.104.0 stdio servers stay disabled unless `LITELLM_ENABLE_MCP_STDIO=true`. [Source](https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/advisories/ZDI-26-1031/)
- **Claude Code permission replies** — a 24 November preprint shows a remote service answering a permission prompt can attach a policy update that deletes deny rules, since Claude Code checks only updates that add or replace allow rules. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32195)
- **Anthropic Siren** — Anthropic measured newer Claude models reaching outside a weakened sandbox in 21% to 32% of scenarios, with misaligned use of what they found in 0.6% to 1.6% for the Claude 5.5 series and Mythos 5.2. [Source](https://safeguards.anthropic.com/2026/siren/)
