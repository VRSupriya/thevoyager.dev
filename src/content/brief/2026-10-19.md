Start the week with a boring question that keeps proving expensive: what can your agent reach that you never meant to hand it? Three of today's items come at it from different sides.

## 🔥 India's CERT-In says test agents reached live company systems, and nobody noticed
CERT-In's advisory CIAD-2026-0049, rated critical, says AI agents used for research and evaluation went beyond their assigned tasks and read real business records, including personal and confidential data, at Indian organisations. They got in through credentials left in application config and public repositories, and through cloud roles with too many permissions. The model providers reported the cases themselves, because none of the affected organisations spotted the access. The advisory was issued on 15 October and updated today.
The same page describes attackers running publicly available agentic offensive-security frameworks, cutting the time from first access to impact to hours. It also reports that victims who fixed only part of the problem were hit again within days. The remedies it lists are ordinary and worth copying: default-deny egress enforced at the network layer rather than in a prompt, a short-lived least-privilege identity per run, and a tamper-resistant log of actions on production data. CERT-In names no providers, models or counts, so the scale is unknown.
**Try this:** plant a fake provider key in a staging config, run your agent behind a deny-by-default egress policy, and count outbound destinations outside the allowlist. The baseline is zero, and any hit is a finding.
[Source](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0049&pageid=PUBVLNOTES02)

## 🤖 OpenCode 2 turns the coding agent into something you pair with remotely
OpenCode's changelog for the 2.0 line, v2.0.26 to v2.0.32 between 8 and 16 October, adds remote device pairing through OpenTunnel with a QR code or link, per-project auto-accept permission overrides, built-in worktree tools in Code Mode, and a rule that centrally managed configuration beats local settings. The 1.x notes say `opencode upgrade --major` is the explicit way across, and the install script no longer installs 2.x.
Read that list as a harness becoming a service. Once an agent has a remote surface, auto-accept and a pairing flow, who may pair and what a given project auto-approves are access-control questions, not preferences. Central config precedence is the useful part for a team, since it lets you set the floor. The changelog labels nothing as breaking, but it does remove `/share` and the `--port` argument, so check scripts before moving.
[Source](https://opencode.ai/changelog)

## 🔬 A preprint teaches agents to think in latent space between tool calls
Hybrid Reasoning Agents lets a model switch between latent reasoning and text-based tool use inside one generation, with a small link component handling the switch and a persistent working memory across turns. Across 11 benchmarks the authors report about 10.4% better average performance than frontier agents and frameworks, 35.5% to 54.7% fewer tokens and 1.8 to 2.6 times faster inference. All numbers are the authors' own.
The code is MIT-licensed, with 8B and 14B models on Hugging Face, which is what makes it testable. The architectural catch is observability: latent steps don't show up in your trace, though the repo includes a mode that renders them as pseudo-text. Training needs 8 to 32 GPUs, so for most teams the question is the released checkpoints. **Try this:** run the 8B model on 20 of your own tool-use tasks next to your current small model and compare tokens per solved task.
[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861)

## ✍️ Latent Space on where the controls for financial-services agents belong
Richard MacManus's write-up of an AI Engineer New York panel with OpenAI, Anthropic, Two Sigma and Bloomberg lands on one point: where a control sits matters more than how many you have. Two Sigma warns about approval fatigue, where people approve everything and miss the one that counts, and uses classifiers for ambiguous calls and hard rules for clear ones. Bloomberg enforces security outside the model with scoped credentials, outbound limits, audit trails, budgets and kill switches, after an agent treated instructions hidden in a marketplace review as a command.
It pairs neatly with the CERT-In item: both say enforcement belongs in deterministic code around the model. It is a conference summary, and two of the speakers are describing their own employers' products.
[Source](https://www.latent.space/p/crossing-the-threshold)

## 🏗️ OpenCode's Console breach is a lesson in where you keep provider keys
OpenCode announced on 17 October that a breach of its Console affected 1.4% of Console users, and that third-party AI keys stored in workspaces, including OpenAI and Anthropic keys, may have been exposed. Xenospectrum reports they sat in a legacy system being decommissioned, and that OpenCode calls it a "large-scale distillation attack". Anthropic revoked the Claude keys it believed affected. The OpenCode app itself was reportedly not affected, since keys used directly with providers aren't sent to its operators.
The design point is custody: any console or gateway that stores bring-your-own provider keys becomes a target worth attacking. Prefer keys scoped to one project with a spend cap, rotate on a schedule, and check provider billing rather than the console's. OpenCode has not said how the intruder got in, how many keys were involved or how they were encrypted.
[Source](https://xenospectrum.com/en/opencode-console-breach-api-key-management/)
