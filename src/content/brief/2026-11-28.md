Two of today's stories come down to the same question: what does the gain look like once you measure end to end? One is a decoder, the other is a skill library.

## 🔥 Tencent open-sources a long-context decoder that rides on the target's cache
Tencent released DFly2 on 26 November, a speculative-decoding drafter that reads the target model's own sparse KV cache instead of keeping a cache of its own. Checkpoints cover DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and Hy4 flash, with vLLM configs for the first two.

```stats
2.87x | average faster decode on long context, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (Tencent's README, concurrency 1)
2.11x | faster end to end on the same run
```

The decode figure is the headline, but the README's own end-to-end column is lower: 1.50x on the agentic test for the same model. Those runs are greedy at concurrency 1, and the paper adds that at high concurrency and matched per-user decode speed, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash throughput is 62% and 81% higher on two 64K test sets.

**Try this:** replay a day of your long-context traces with and without DFly2 on one supported target. Measure end-to-end p50 latency at your real concurrency, not tokens per second. Check the LICENSE file first: the repo doesn't name one in its README.

[Source](https://github.com/Tencent/AngelSpec) · [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34776)

## 🚀 GigaChat4 arrives with MIT weights and a million-token window
Sber's ai-sage put GigaChat4 on Hugging Face on 20 November in two sizes. The 708B model activates 41B parameters per token, carries a 1,048,576-token context and uses an MIT licence; the 37B-A4B one runs on a single H100 80GB but is licensed for non-commercial use by individuals only.

The notable part is the architecture: a 3:1 mix of Gated DeltaNet linear attention and DeepSeek Sparse Attention, which is how a million tokens stays affordable. The developers report 80.89 on SWE-bench Verified in reasoning mode, which is their own number. SGLang supports it natively, but vLLM needs a pinned commit rather than a release, and the big model wants two 8xH100 nodes. For a self-hosted long-context coding agent it's worth a look. For anything else, wait for independent runs.

[Source](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat4-708B-A41B) · [Model card, 37B-A4B](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat4-37B-A4B) · [Repo](https://github.com/salute-developers/gigachat4) · [Release date](https://huggingface.co/api/models/ai-sage/GigaChat4-708B-A41B) · [37B licence](https://huggingface.co/ai-sage/GigaChat4-37B-A4B/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)

## 🤖 PyTorch lets an AI reviewer approve pull requests, with gates
GreenLight, the open-source cousin of Meta's RADAR, has started approving PRs from PyTorch maintainers who already hold approval rights. Everyone else gets shadow-mode reviews that never become approvals. It scans every five minutes as an AWS Lambda, and a reverted PR is dropped from every path permanently.

What's worth stealing is the gating. Approval depends on a diff-risk percentile that differs by author, per InfoQ: allowlisted runbooks up to the 50th, other bots up to the 20th, and human-authored diffs by default only the 5th. The RADAR paper also asks for a review confidence of at least 8 out of 10. Trust here comes from provenance and track record, not from the model sounding sure. If you're letting agents near your merge button, write down that table first.

[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/uber-agent-spend-steady/) · [GreenLight repo](https://github.com/pytorch/test-infra/tree/main/greenlight)

## 🔬 An audit says SkillsBench's skill lift was overstated
SkillsBench reported that curated skills raise agent pass rates by about 16.6 percentage points. A preprint posted on 25 November went through the source and 7,554 public trials and found why that number is shaky: four tasks had placeholder skill folders, the harness added 48 built-in skills in most runs, and with at most three runs per condition, 58% of cells couldn't have shown a decrease. A gating strategy that seemed to gain 2-3 points still showed 4-7 after labels were shuffled.

Code and audit ledgers are public, so you can rerun it. The takeaway for your own evals is cheap: keep a no-skill control, repeat each condition more than three times, and don't trust a lift that comes from three tasks.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33943)

## ✍️ How Anthropic runs reviews and on-call with Claude
Gergely Orosz's 25 November piece lists how Anthropic works day to day, as the company describes it. An internal Claude classifier triages every pull request: whether a human must look, how many reviewers, and which team. A Claude agent is paged before humans for API on-call and filters alerts into incidents or noise. Teams stay at five to eight people and keep their own on-call rotations.

The interesting design choice is where humans stay in the loop: at the edges of review and paging, not inside them. Whether the coordination cost really stays flat is the open question; the piece says planning takes about as long as before.

[Source](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-anthropic-builds-software-in)

## 🏗️ Google gives Workspace agents a single door, and a date
In a post dated 24 November, Google said AI agents and similar tools must use its Workspace MCP servers or the Workspace CLI rather than calling the REST APIs directly. Technical enforcement begins on 1 February 2027, and billing for the new Scaled tier starts on 1 April 2027; the Standard tier has no cost and published quotas. The servers and CLI are generally available, rolling out to all domains over about two weeks.

For architects the point is the choke point. One governed path gives you audit and policy, and one set of quotas you can budget. It also ties your agents to the tool surface and limits Google exposes. If any of your agents hit Gmail, Drive or Calendar directly, that list is your migration backlog.

**Try this:** grep your agent code for direct Workspace API calls, move the busiest one to the MCP server, and compare error rate and latency against the old path before the deadline.

[Source](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/mcp-servers-cli-standardized-usage-tiers-now-available.html)
