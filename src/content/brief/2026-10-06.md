Today's stories share a quiet theme: the boring layer under the AI work, whether that's a disk driver, a TLS certificate or a memory index, is where the interesting failures live.

## 🔥 Cloudflare leaked leftover disk data between container tenants
Cloudflare disclosed that Containers ran Linux dm-thin with `skip_block_zeroing` on. When a 64 KiB block was reassigned and a customer wrote only 4 KiB, the rest still held the previous owner's bytes. A researcher reported it on September 4. The fix landed within hours, and the last cached snapshots were purged by September 19. Cloudflare says it found no evidence of exploitation, and attackers couldn't choose a victim.
If you run agent sandboxes or code-execution tools on shared hosts, this is the failure to test for: tenant isolation that holds at the process level and leaks at the block level. Ask your provider how disks are scrubbed between tenants, then check it yourself with a partial-write test.
[Source](https://blog.cloudflare.com/containers-cross-tenant-vulnerability/)

## 🚀 Gemini 4 Argon is out, for a very small audience
Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30. For now it's open only to vetted cyber defenders through its Fairwind program, with wider access "coming soon" and no date. Google's table has it ahead of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 on 13 of 19 benchmarks, 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, and a 1 million token output limit. Introductory pricing is $2 in and $10 out per million tokens, rising to $4 and $20.
Those are Google's own comparisons, and The Rundown reports no third party had reproduced them at launch. The long output limit and the 95% cached-input discount are the parts that would change a pipeline, so plan a bake-off once real access opens rather than rewriting anything today.
[Source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/)

## 🤖 An open-source inbox for agents you don't want to watch
Pizza Bot is an Apache 2.0 app where you hand work to agents and review results later in an inbox with All, Unread and Action queues. Agents can run on schedules or webhooks, delegate to specialist workers and pause for human approval. State, checkpoints and logs stay in a local folder, and data leaves only toward your configured model providers and enabled MCP servers.
The pattern worth borrowing is the approval pause as a first-class queue, not a chat interrupt. It's a community project with no SLA, though, so treat it as a reference design for async agent UX, not something to put in front of customers.
[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/pizza-bot-ai-agents/)

## 🔬 Agent memory that digs only as deep as the question
APDMem (EMNLP 2026 industry track) stores conversation memory in four layers: themes, personal key facts, turn-level notes and raw messages. A controller starts at the top and drills down only when the query needs it. On LongMemEval the authors report touching just 8% of the conversation.
That's a latency and token-cost story, and it makes the controller's stopping decision the new failure point. I'd want to see how it behaves when a summary is confidently wrong, and the abstract doesn't say.
[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02472)

## ✍️ Andrew Ng: treat AI security as an engineering problem
In this week's Batch letter, Ng compares AI security to aviation safety and argues that cyber and agent-containment risks improve with systematic engineering. The issue also covers Anthropic's analysis of open-weight GLM-5.3: 12% on ExploitBench against 14% for Claude Mythos, and $20.40 of GLM-5.3-Flash tokens was enough to find a recently disclosed Chrome vulnerability.
The second number is the one for your threat model. Cheap, open exploit capability means you can't count on attackers being expensive, so patch speed and containment belong in the design, not the postmortem.
[Source](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-373)

## 🏗️ Cloudflare wants to run a free CA for quantum-safe certificates
Cloudflare plans a public CA issuing Merkle Tree Certificates, using ML-DSA and Falcon signatures. The CA signs a tree root and each certificate carries a short inclusion proof, which avoids the roughly 40x handshake growth of dropping post-quantum signatures into X.509. Public issuance is expected in early 2027, after production experiments with Chrome.
Servers will need to present both classic and Merkle certificates for a while, and validity periods become tied to tree updates. That makes ACME automation a hard prerequisite, so check how many of your internal and service-mesh certs still renew by hand.
[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/postquatam-certificates/)
