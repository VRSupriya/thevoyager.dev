Today's thread is control: who decides when your agent platform changes, and how much of what you measure is just drift.

## 🔥 Self-hosted Managed Agents arrives with an 8-hour rollback clause
Anthropic's docs now describe self-hosted Claude Managed Agents: you run the server, its PostgreSQL 16+ database and the sandboxes, while the model runs at Anthropic or at a provider you choose (Bedrock, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry or your own gateway). Access goes through your Anthropic account team, and the licence took effect on 12 November.

The licence is the interesting part. If Anthropic notifies you in writing that a version is a safety, security or misuse risk, you must disable or roll it back in every deployment within 8 hours. Safety Updates must be deployed within 1 business day, and agent incidents reported within 48 hours of confirming them.

Self-hosting here buys data location, not independence. Put the 8-hour window into your on-call and change-freeze design before you sign.

[Source](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/self-hosted) · [Licence](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/self-hosted-managed-agents-license)

## 🚀 Edge0-145B: a 145B coder that claims to fit one gaming GPU
Edge0-145B-NVFP4 appeared on Hugging Face on 11 November. Its card describes a 145B sparse mixture-of-experts model with 10.12B active parameters per token and a 256K context, trained natively at 4 bits on top of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next.

The card claims 98.3 tokens a second of agent decode on one RTX 5090 D with 24 GB, and 86.5 on Terminal-Bench 2.1. Both are claims on the card. The licence is a custom Edge0 community licence, with the Qwen licence still applying to Qwen materials.

If an agent loop needs dozens of cheap calls, a local model that fast changes the cost sums. Read the licence before you plan on it.

**Try this:** run 20 tasks from your own repo through your current coder and through this one. Compare wall-clock time per accepted patch, not leaderboard scores.

[Source](https://huggingface.co/Edge0/Edge0-145B-NVFP4)

## 🤖 "Pinned" MCP servers kept changing underneath
Corelight published research on 10 November showing that pinning an MCP server's release doesn't pin what it depends on. One pinned server ran with 15 different dependency sets over five weeks, and 23 of its 69 packages changed, including a cryptography library and the MCP SDK. Of 35 servers reviewed, 23 recommended an install that pulls from a public registry at each launch, install or build.

The fix Corelight suggests is to freeze the environment once: install from a hash-pinned lock file, launch directly and re-lock on a schedule. Five frozen servers kept identical package lists across 457 starts.

**Affected:** servers launched through unlocked uvx or npx installs; none are named · **Fixed in:** no patch applies; Corelight's remedy is to freeze the environment · **Severity:** not stated · **Stopgap:** freeze the environment from a lock file and scope each server's credentials · **If compromised:** not stated

[Source](https://corelight.com/blog/mcp-server-supply-chain-risk) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/13/corelight-mcp-server-supply-chain/)

## 🔬 Your quantization comparison has a noise floor
A preprint submitted on 11 November measures what changes when you compare a quantized model with its BF16 original, besides the weights. On 2,400 public decisions from two decision models run on an NVIDIA GB10, non-quantization sources (engine, restart, batch, kernel) moved between 0 and 0.92% of decisions. Quantization flipped 0.88% to 2.12% of decisions at 8 bits (Q8_0 and FP8) and 3.50% to 5.79% at 4 bits (Q4_K_M and NVFP4), across the two models.

So 8-bit differences sit near the noise and 4-bit ones clear it. The code and recipes are released, and the data partly (some sets as IDs, private sets only as aggregates). It used two Cloudflare decision models, so check your own workload before copying the thresholds.

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15671) · [Code and data](https://github.com/Level5Tech/clef-quant-bench)

## 🏗️ Netflix puts an ontology under its incident triage
InfoQ reported on 13 November that Netflix built an ontology-driven knowledge graph so that logs, metrics and traces share one vocabulary. Netflix says it handles more than 2 billion requests a day and about 38 million logging events a second; InfoQ puts a typical regression investigation at around four hours. LLM-assisted "enrichers" produce the triples, and a correlation engine examines 13 signal types.

The design choice worth copying is that the agent talks to a shared model of the system, with humans meant to keep approval authority. The cost is that someone must own the ontology, and InfoQ doesn't say whether any of it is released.

[Source](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/netflix-ai-driven-observability/)
